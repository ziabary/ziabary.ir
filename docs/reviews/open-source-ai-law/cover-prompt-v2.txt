Use case: stylized-concept
Asset: replacement cover for a Persian essay about open-source and open knowledge as the foundations of AI, and the need for effective legal support in Iran. Wide 16:9 landscape.
Primary request: a richly detailed, crowded editorial scene whose subject is unmistakably OPEN SOURCE AND OPEN LICENSING at first glance. Recognizable real open-source and open-knowledge symbols must be the visual protagonists, not decorative details.
Composition: one continuous panoramic scene with foreground-to-background storytelling. In the foreground is a large, geographically recognizable Iran-shaped work surface, seen from a high three-quarter angle. It is covered with a lively, disordered assortment of license plaques, source-code tiles, data cards, research pages, image and audio assets, and open hardware circuit boards. These valuable resources look neglected and legally unprotected: loosened attribution tags, scattered license sheets, and gaps in an unfinished protective frame around the Iran-shaped surface. Some resources are already being used on this surface; do NOT portray Iran as empty or devoid of technology. No damaged national flag, no people to caricature. The problem is an incomplete legal framework, not a lack of intelligence or ownership.
In the connected middle and far background, the SAME open-license and open-knowledge elements are visibly assembled into the foundations and structural layers of a flourishing worldwide AI ecosystem: transparent model matrices, document-retrieval stacks, scientific workbenches, server clusters, a world network/globe. Clear physical connections run from source, data, content and hardware blocks into the AI systems. This must communicate that the world is BUILDING ON these resources, while legal protection for them remains unfinished in the Iranian foreground. Do not depict resources being stolen or extracted from Iran, and do not imply that the world's open source originates only in Iran.
Prominent recognizable icons: the green Open Source Initiative keyhole-ring symbol; Creative Commons circled CC plus the BY attribution and SA share-alike symbols; the GNU gnu head; the Linux Tux penguin. Integrate recognizable Wikimedia/Wikipedia knowledge globe and OpenStreetMap map-and-magnifier symbols to make clear this goes beyond software. Include a few smaller Python, PyTorch and Arduino project symbols among the infrastructure; do not confuse project names with license names.
Prominent physical license plaques with crisp, exact short lettering, each in a different position: "MIT", "Apache 2.0", "GPLv3", "BSD", "CC BY", "CC BY-SA", "ODbL", "OFL", "CERN-OHL". Organize enough visual hierarchy that at least OSI, CC, GNU and several license names remain readable in a small cover thumbnail. Open content, databases, typefaces and open hardware must have visible objects as well as their license plaques. Only these short labels; no headline or long invented text. A small unobtrusive "AI" on the background structure is acceptable.
Style: sophisticated editorial 3D collage with tactile printed paper, enamel logo plaques and hardware; busy, layered, slightly satirical, warm and intellectually sharp. A human-designed magazine cover, not generic science-fiction architecture. Large clear symbols in the foreground, intricate details in the background. Teal and ink-blue setting, warm ivory paper, authentic green OSI and natural logo colors, copper/amber lighting.
Constraints: no giant book, no courthouse, no scales, no gavel, no padlock, no robot head, no glowing brain, no generic central cube. No company endorsements, no political slogans, no watermark. Do not call the resources public domain or ownerless; they have creators and licenses. Do not depict all Creative Commons licenses as interchangeable. No NC or ND license badges. The point is open cooperation supported by enforceable rights. Fill the frame with recognizable open-source/open-knowledge culture and licenses while preserving a clear visual story.
